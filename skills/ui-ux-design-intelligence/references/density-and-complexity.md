# Density and Complexity

## Principle

Complex products are not improved by hiding all complexity.

The goal is **managed complexity**.

## Match density to work

High-density interfaces are appropriate when:
- users are experienced;
- comparison matters;
- tasks repeat frequently;
- screen real estate is valuable.

Lower density is appropriate when:
- tasks are infrequent;
- guidance matters;
- emotional pacing matters;
- touch dominates.

## Progressive disclosure

Use for advanced/rare options.

Do not hide frequently used controls behind repeated overflow.

## Chunking

Group information by:
- task;
- object;
- decision.

Avoid sections based only on backend ownership.

## Tables

For large comparable datasets, tables are often superior to card grids.

Cards can make scanning/comparison slower.

## Expert modes

Consider:
- compact density;
- keyboard shortcuts;
- saved views;
- bulk actions;
- column customization.

## Cognitive load

Reduce unnecessary:
- choices;
- terminology;
- context switching;
- visual noise.

Do not reduce useful information merely to make screenshots look cleaner.
