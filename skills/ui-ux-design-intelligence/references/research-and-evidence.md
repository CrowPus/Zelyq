# Research and Evidence

## Evidence levels

Distinguish:
1. standard/requirement;
2. platform convention;
3. established heuristic;
4. domain research;
5. product analytics/support evidence;
6. qualitative usability research;
7. designer hypothesis.

Do not present a hypothesis as research.

## Product research

Useful evidence:
- interviews;
- usability tests;
- support tickets;
- analytics funnels;
- search logs;
- session replays with privacy safeguards;
- surveys;
- sales/customer-success feedback.

Each source has bias.

## Prototype before certainty

When interaction risk is high:
- create low/medium fidelity prototype;
- test key tasks;
- observe failures;
- revise.

## Usability test focus

Test tasks, not whether participants "like the design."

Observe:
- success;
- time/effort;
- hesitation;
- errors;
- recovery;
- comprehension.

## Domain research

Use strong external research only when context matches.

Example: Baymard's e-commerce research is highly relevant to checkout/search/product browsing, not automatically to B2B observability dashboards.

## Decision log

For major choices record:
- decision;
- evidence;
- alternatives;
- tradeoff;
- confidence;
- validation plan.
