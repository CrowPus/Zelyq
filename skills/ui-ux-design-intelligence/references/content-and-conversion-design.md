# Content and Conversion Design

## Conversion is not coercion

A conversion interface should help qualified users decide and act with confidence.

Avoid:
- fake scarcity;
- disguised ads;
- confirmshaming;
- hidden cancellation;
- misleading button hierarchy;
- preselected consent with unclear meaning.

## Landing-page hierarchy

A useful sequence often answers:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why does it matter?
4. How does it work?
5. Why trust it?
6. What should I do?

The exact section order depends on product and audience.

## Proof

Use real:
- customer names/logos with permission;
- metrics with provenance;
- product screenshots;
- outcomes;
- security/compliance claims.

Do not invent proof to make a mockup look complete.

## CTA

Primary CTA should match commitment level.

Examples:
- "Start free"
- "Book demo"
- "View pricing"

Avoid forcing high-commitment CTA before enough information exists.

## Pricing

Make:
- billing period;
- included limits;
- overages;
- cancellation/trial terms

clear.

## Review

- Does the page communicate value before style?
- Is proof credible?
- Are claims specific?
- Is CTA appropriate to buyer stage?
