# Usability Heuristics

Use Nielsen Norman Group's 10 heuristics as a structured critique method. They are broad heuristics, not absolute laws.

## 1. Visibility of system status
Keep users informed with timely feedback.

Questions:
- What is happening?
- Did the action work?
- Is the system waiting, saving, processing, or done?

## 2. Match between system and real world
Use domain/user language and natural ordering.

## 3. User control and freedom
Provide exits, cancel, undo, back, and recovery where appropriate.

## 4. Consistency and standards
Similar things should look/behave similarly. Follow platform conventions.

## 5. Error prevention
Prevent high-cost mistakes before they occur.

## 6. Recognition rather than recall
Expose needed options/context instead of requiring memory.

## 7. Flexibility and efficiency
Support acceleration for experienced users without harming novices.

## 8. Aesthetic and minimalist design
Every extra element competes with relevant information. Minimalist means relevant, not empty.

## 9. Help users recognize, diagnose, recover from errors
Use plain language, identify the problem, and provide recovery.

## 10. Help and documentation
Prefer self-explanatory design, but provide contextual help when tasks genuinely need it.

## Severity review

For each issue score:
- frequency;
- impact;
- persistence;
- recoverability.

Prioritize high-impact/repeated failures over cosmetic preference.

## Evidence discipline

A heuristic violation is a reasoned design concern, not proof that users fail.

When a decision is high-stakes or ambiguous:
- prototype;
- usability test;
- inspect analytics/support evidence;
- state uncertainty.
