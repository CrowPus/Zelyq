## Emergency record statuses

| Status | Meaning | Work allowed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposed | Emergency record is being prepared; no containment authority is recorded | No |
| Active containment | Immediate temporary containment is authorized | Yes, within the recorded emergency boundary |
| Stabilized pending retrospective | Immediate harm is contained; retrospective review is due | Stabilization, rollback, and evidence preservation only |
| Retrospective review | Permanent decision and lessons are under review | No new permanent work without a separate authorization |
| Closed | Containment and required retrospective actions are complete | No |
| Superseded | A successor entry or policy governs remaining work | Only under the successor |

## Active harm or obligation

Describe the security incident, data-loss risk, legal violation, or material service failure requiring urgent action.

## Evidence available

Record verified observations, uncertainty, affected systems or people, and source timestamps.

## Reviewer and authority declarations

Record the emergency authority and each reviewer’s role, relevant competence, relationship or conflict, independence limits, materials reviewed, and decision time. When normal independent review is impossible, record why and set the retrospective review deadline.

## Why prior review is unsafe

Explain why delay would materially increase harm and which normal gates cannot be completed first.

## Authorized containment

Define the smallest temporary action, responsible person, environment, time limit, monitoring, verification, and rollback.

When action preceded the record, explain why and record it as soon as the situation is stable. Change status to `Active containment` only after the emergency authority, decision, boundary, and expiration or review date are recorded. Emergency authority expires at the recorded deadline and does not authorize permanent adoption.

## Outcome, closure, and retrospective review

Record actions taken, results, deviations, remaining risk, incident evidence, required research, permanent-decision entry, rollback or cleanup, and the decision to close or supersede the record.
