# The Engineering Research Book

## Part III synthesis — Teams, projects, and engineering memory

Part ID: ERB-03

Version: 0.1

Status: Reviewed — Part exit criterion passed with limitations

Last substantive review: 2026-08-25

## Synthesis

Software teams create capabilities no isolated contributor possesses. They coordinate interdependent changes, challenge decisions, route questions to expertise, preserve rationale, grant authority, maintain systems, respond to incidents, and revise organizational practice. These capabilities arise only when social and technical mechanisms remain aligned with the dependencies of the work.

Communication is useful when it delivers relevant information to affected actors in time and in a form that can guide action. Review is useful when scope, context, expertise, and responsibility make consequential claims inspectable. A meeting count, approval, or message volume is not an outcome.

Engineering memory has at least four layers:

1. artifacts preserving facts, decisions, evidence, histories, and procedures;
2. people holding tacit discrimination, context, and skill;
3. a transactive map of where expertise and authority can be found; and
4. executable controls—tests, monitors, automation, safeguards, and permissions—that apply prior learning.

Every layer can decay. Documents become stale, people leave, expertise maps become wrong, and controls stop matching the environment. Durable memory therefore requires ownership, retrieval during real decisions, validation, correction, and transparent supersession.

## Individual and collective boundary

An individual can reason well yet fail without access, coordination, authority, or feedback. A team can produce a good outcome despite one member’s error when defenses detect and recover. Conversely, multiple reviewers or implementations may share blind spots. Evaluation must attribute capability and responsibility at the level where the mechanism actually exists.

## Conditions for continuity

The reviewed findings support these bounded conditions:

- dependencies and affected parties are identifiable;
- information can reach the people and artifacts that depend on it;
- consequential decisions retain rationale, assumptions, evidence, and revision triggers;
- tacit knowledge has transfer paths and critical roles have continuity;
- newcomers gain authority through bounded, observed participation;
- ownership joins knowledge, duty, authority, backup, and escalation;
- maintenance is evaluated through future change outcomes; and
- incident learning changes and verifies capability rather than ending at a report.

## Requirements carried forward

Part IV must not evaluate an AI system as if repository access alone supplies team context or authority. It must test interaction with reviews, ownership, decision memory, coordination, and incident feedback. Part V must examine how reliance changes collective accountability and whether automation obscures knowledge loss or correlated failure.

## Limitations and exit decision

Evidence is heterogeneous and often observational or organization-specific. It underrepresents small organizations, non-Western contexts, safety-critical domains, accessibility, adversarial coordination, and long-term comparative outcomes. Within these limits, Part III meets its exit criterion by distinguishing individual capabilities from those emerging through teams, processes, tools, and organizations.

This synthesis does not create a Zelyq requirement or authorize code.
