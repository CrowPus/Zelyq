# The Engineering Research Book

# Contribution Guide

Version: 1.0

Status: Active

---

# Welcome

Thank you for contributing to The Engineering Research Book.

By contributing to this project, you are helping improve our collective understanding of software engineering and AI-assisted engineering.

This project is built on one belief:

> Great software begins with great understanding.

Every contribution, whether large or small, should help us better understand software engineering.

---

# Before You Contribute

Before writing a new page or modifying an existing one, ask yourself:

- What question am I trying to answer?
- Has this question already been researched?
- Do I have enough evidence?
- Can I support my conclusions?
- Am I improving understanding?

If the answer to these questions is unclear, spend more time researching before writing.

---

# Who Can Contribute?

Anyone.

You do not need to be a senior engineer.

You do not need a computer science degree.

You do not need years of industry experience.

Good research comes from curiosity, careful thinking, and intellectual honesty.

Every contribution will be evaluated on the quality of its reasoning and evidence, not on the background of its author.

---

# What Makes a Good Contribution?

A good contribution:

- asks an important question
- improves understanding
- is supported by evidence
- clearly separates facts from opinions
- considers alternative explanations
- acknowledges uncertainty
- improves the quality of the book

Research should add clarity.

Not complexity.

---

# What Should You Avoid?

Avoid:

- unsupported claims
- marketing language
- personal attacks
- copying content without proper attribution
- writing features instead of researching problems
- treating opinions as facts

The purpose of this book is to understand software engineering.

Not to promote Zelyq.

---

# Challenge Ideas, Not People

Healthy disagreement is encouraged.

Respectful discussion improves research.

When reviewing someone else's work:

Challenge assumptions.

Question evidence.

Suggest improvements.

Provide alternative perspectives.

Always remain respectful.

---

# If You Disagree

Disagreement is valuable.

When disagreeing:

Explain why.

Provide evidence.

Suggest an alternative interpretation.

Avoid emotional arguments.

Our goal is not agreement.

Our goal is better understanding.

---

# Improving Existing Chapters

Whenever possible,

improve existing chapters before creating new ones.

Knowledge should accumulate.

Not become fragmented.

Before opening a new chapter, ask:

Would improving an existing chapter better serve the reader?

---

# Writing New Research

Every new research chapter should begin with a question.

Examples:

What activities consume most of a senior engineer's time?

Why do engineering teams lose architectural knowledge?

How do developers build trust in AI systems?

Avoid beginning with proposed solutions.

The purpose of research is to discover answers.

Not defend ideas.

---

# Updating Research

Research evolves.

When new evidence becomes available:

Update the chapter.

Revise the conclusions if necessary.

Explain why changes were made.

Changing conclusions because of better evidence is considered progress.

---

# Respect Intellectual Honesty

One of the most important responsibilities of every contributor is intellectual honesty.

If evidence contradicts your original belief,

change your belief.

Do not ignore evidence to protect previous conclusions.

Truth is more valuable than consistency.

---

# Questions Are Valuable

Not every contribution needs to provide answers.

Identifying important unanswered questions is itself a valuable contribution.

Good research begins with good questions.

---

# Our Shared Responsibility

Every contributor helps protect the credibility of this project.

Future engineers may rely on this research.

Write carefully.

Research honestly.

Review respectfully.

Improve continuously.

---

# Thank You

Thank you for helping build a body of knowledge that aims to improve software engineering through careful research, open collaboration, and thoughtful discussion.

Your contribution is not simply improving a document.

It is helping build a shared understanding that others can learn from, challenge, and continue to improve.
