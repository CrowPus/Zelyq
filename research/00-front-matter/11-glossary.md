# The Engineering Research Book

## Glossary

Version: 1.0

Status: Active — Version 1 reviewed

---

## Purpose

This glossary defines terms that carry a specific or recurring meaning in The Engineering Research Book. Its purpose is consistency, not completeness.

The definitions are operational: they state how the foundation currently uses a term. A research chapter may examine, qualify, or replace a definition when evidence shows that a different account is required. When a chapter uses a term differently, it must identify the difference and explain why.

Definitions should not be treated as research findings unless they cite completed research. Terms omitted from this glossary retain their ordinary disciplinary meaning in context.

## A

**AI engineering system**
An artificial-intelligence-based system that performs or assists with one or more activities involved in software engineering. The term does not imply autonomy, human equivalence, reliability, or trustworthiness.

**AI software engineer**
A shorthand description of the research subject: an AI system proposed to participate across meaningful software-engineering responsibilities. It is not treated as an established professional category or as evidence that AI can replace a human engineer.

**Analysis**
The reasoned examination of evidence to identify patterns, relationships, explanations, differences, and uncertainty. Analysis interprets evidence; it does not create observations that were not collected.

**Anecdote**
A report of a single event or limited personal experience that was not produced through a systematic research design. An anecdote may generate a question, reveal a possibility, or counter a universal claim, but it does not establish prevalence or typicality by itself.

**Approval gate**
A required review checkpoint that must reach a recorded decision before an engineering application entry can advance. Passing one gate does not imply that other gates have passed.

**Artifact**
An object created, used, or examined during engineering or research. Examples include software, source code, models, datasets, protocols, instruments, analysis scripts, and evidence tables. See also *research artifact*.

**Assumption**
A proposition treated as provisionally true for the purpose of an argument, design, or study. Material assumptions must be stated and should not be presented as findings.

## B

**Benchmark**
A defined collection of tasks, data, conditions, and measures used to compare or track system performance. A benchmark result applies directly only to the evaluated setup and does not automatically establish performance in broader engineering work.

**Body of evidence**
The collection of relevant evidence assessed together for one specific finding. A body of evidence is evaluated for bias, consistency, directness, precision or adequacy, coverage, independence, and missing evidence.

## C

**Calibrated reliance**
Reliance that corresponds appropriately to a system's demonstrated capability, limitations, context, and consequence of failure. Calibration concerns whether reliance is warranted, not merely whether a person feels confident.

**Capability**
The demonstrated ability of a person, team, organization, or system to produce a defined outcome under stated conditions. Capability claims require the task, conditions, measures, and relevant limitations to be identified.

**Causal claim**
A claim that a change in one factor produces or contributes to a change in another. Causal claims require evidence and analysis capable of addressing plausible alternative causes.

**Chapter**
The primary unit of published inquiry in the book. A chapter answers one primary research question through a traceable method, evidence base, analysis, findings, and limitations.

**Claim**
A statement presented as true or supportable. Material claims must be connected to evidence or to an explicit chain of reasoning.

**Claim–evidence record**
The traceable record connecting a material finding to its scope, supporting and contradictory evidence, appraisal, confidence, limitations, and review date.

**Confidence**
The degree to which the assessed body of evidence supports a specific finding within its stated scope. The book uses High, Moderate, Low, and Insufficient confidence. Confidence is distinct from importance, desirability, and the confidence felt by a user.

**Conflict of interest**
A relationship, incentive, or commitment that could reasonably affect, or appear to affect, research judgment. Disclosure enables appropriate scrutiny but does not automatically invalidate a contribution.

**Constraint**
A condition that limits acceptable decisions or actions. Constraints may be technical, organizational, legal, ethical, financial, operational, temporal, or related to user needs.

**Context**
The conditions surrounding evidence, work, or system behavior that may affect interpretation or transfer. Context can include participants, organization, task, software, tools, version, incentives, environment, time, and consequence of failure.

## D

**Data provenance**
The documented origin, collection history, ownership, transformations, and custody of data used in research.

**Design implication**
A possible consequence of a finding for evaluation, policy, workflow, or system design. An implication contains reasoning beyond the finding and is not itself evidence or an automatic product requirement.

**Directness**
The degree to which evidence matches the population, system, setting, task, outcome, and period named in a claim. Evidence becomes more indirect as additional extrapolation is required.

## E

**Edition**
A named, stable release containing specified versions of the book's chapters and governing material. The living research may continue to change after an edition is released.

**Engineering judgment**
The process of reaching and revising decisions about software under uncertainty, constraint, and consequence. The term does not presume that judgment belongs only to individuals or that experience always makes it reliable.

**Engineering application layer**
The part of the book that applies reviewed research to verified Zelyq problems and context through governed engineering entries, policies, experiments, authorizations, and outcome evaluations. Its records are project decisions, not research findings.

**Engineering application record**
A governed record connecting research and project evidence to a Zelyq-specific problem, options, decision, risk review, implementation boundary, authorization, or evaluation.

**Evidence**
Information used to support, qualify, or challenge a claim. Its strength depends on its relationship to the claim, how it was produced, and the limitations of the relevant source and body of evidence.

**Evidence appraisal**
The structured evaluation of whether evidence can support a particular use. It considers dimensions such as relevance, methodological fit, execution, bias, directness, adequacy, transparency, independence, and recency.

**Evidence synthesis**
A method for identifying, appraising, and combining evidence relevant to a defined question. Forms include systematic reviews, mapping studies, scoping reviews, rapid reviews, meta-analyses, and structured narrative syntheses.

**Experiment entry**
A time-bounded engineering application record authorizing isolated work intended to produce evidence. It does not authorize production adoption of the experimental result.

**Exploratory research**
Research intended to map a topic, develop concepts, identify candidate evidence, or generate hypotheses when the problem or field is not yet sufficiently defined for confirmatory claims.

**External validity**
The extent to which a result may apply beyond the studied population, system, task, or setting. External validity is not established by sample size alone.

## F

**Finding**
The book's bounded answer to a research question, supported by an assessed body of evidence or an explicit conceptual argument. A material finding includes scope, confidence, limitations, and traceability.

**Foundation**
The front matter and governing documents that define the book's purpose, scope, structure, methodology, evidence rules, editorial standards, licensing, and contribution process. The foundation does not include substantive answers to the planned research questions.

## G

**Generalization**
The extension of a finding beyond the evidence directly studied. Generalization requires a reasoned account of why the finding should transfer and where it may not.

**Governing question**
The highest-level question that determines the relevance and organization of the book: what an AI system would need to understand, do, and demonstrate to earn trust as a software engineering partner.

## H

**Hallucination**
AI-generated content presented without adequate grounding in the relevant source, context, or reality. The term should be operationalized in evaluations rather than used as a catch-all label for every system error.

**Human–AI collaboration**
Work in which people and AI systems contribute to a shared activity or outcome. The phrase does not imply equal agency, authority, responsibility, or capability.

**Human oversight**
Human monitoring, review, authorization, intervention, or accountability applied to AI-assisted activity. Oversight is effective only when people have the information, capability, authority, time, and incentives needed to affect outcomes.

**Hypothesis**
A provisional, testable explanation or prediction. A hypothesis organizes inquiry but is not a finding until evaluated through an appropriate method.

## I

**Implication**
A reasoned consequence drawn from one or more findings. Implications may concern later research, evaluation, governance, or design and should identify the additional assumptions connecting them to the findings.

**Implementation authorization**
A recorded decision permitting bounded code work after the required problem, research, option, consequence, and evaluation gates have passed. Authorization applies only to the stated boundary and conditions.

**Independence of evidence**
The degree to which sources rely on distinct data, methods, authorship, incentives, and analytical decisions. Multiple publications based on the same dataset or claim do not provide fully independent corroboration.

**Inference**
A conclusion reached from evidence or premises through reasoning. Material inferential steps should be visible, particularly when they connect findings to broader implications.

## M

**Material claim**
A claim whose removal or correction could change a finding, confidence rating, implication, decision, or reader's understanding of a consequential issue.

**Mental model**
An internal representation used to understand, explain, predict, or act upon a system. The adequacy of a mental model depends on the task and context rather than on whether it reproduces every system detail.

**Method**
The specific procedure used to collect, produce, analyze, or synthesize evidence for a research question.

**Methodology**
The rationale and framework governing the selection, application, and evaluation of research methods. In this book, “Research Methodology” also names the governing process from proposal through continuing review.

## O

**Observation date**
The date on which changeable information—such as model behavior, product functionality, policy, or price—was directly observed or verified.

**Open question**
A relevant question that the available research has not answered adequately. An open question may result from missing evidence, unresolved disagreement, unsuitable methods, or exclusion from current scope.

**Open-source research**
Research whose publication, methods, evidence records, and reusable artifacts are made inspectable and adaptable to the extent permitted by ethics, privacy, security, consent, and licensing. Public access alone does not guarantee rigor.

## P

**Part**
A major field of inquiry within the book, defined by a central question, included scope, dependencies, planned chapters, and an exit criterion.

**Precision**
The degree to which quantitative evidence distinguishes among materially different values or effects. In this book, qualitative evidence is assessed for adequacy rather than forced into a quantitative concept of precision.

**Primary research**
Research that produces or analyzes original data through a stated method.

**Project evidence**
Inspected evidence establishing current facts about Zelyq, its users, code, behavior, architecture, incidents, measurements, constraints, or obligations. Project evidence does not automatically support general claims beyond that context.

**Protocol**
A plan defined before full evidence collection or analysis that records the question, scope, sources or participants, selection, measures, procedure, appraisal, analysis, safeguards, and expected limitations.

**Protocol deviation**
A departure from an approved research protocol. A material deviation records what changed, why, when, what information was available, and how the change may affect the findings.

## R

**Reliability**
The consistency of a result, measure, process, or system under defined conditions. Reliability does not by itself establish validity, usefulness, safety, or trustworthiness.

**Reliance**
The act of depending on a person, process, or system in making a decision or performing work. Reliance may be appropriate or inappropriate regardless of whether trust is consciously reported.

**Replication**
A study that tests an earlier result through a new execution of a method, often using new data or conditions. The exact meaning should be stated because research communities distinguish replication and reproduction differently.

**Reproducibility**
The ability to regenerate a result using the relevant original data, artifacts, procedure, and environment. The term must identify what was reproduced and under which conditions.

**Research artifact**
A protocol, dataset, instrument, search record, coding guide, analysis script, environment description, review record, or other material that supports inspection or reproduction of research.

**Research question**
A precise, bounded question that a chapter investigates through evidence, analysis, or explicit conceptual reasoning. A research question must permit more than one possible answer.

**Risk**
The possibility of an outcome combined with its consequences and relevant uncertainty. A chapter should define the dimensions and measures of risk appropriate to its context.

**Risk of bias**
The possibility that systematic features of research design, execution, analysis, reporting, incentives, or missing evidence distort a result. It is an assessment of vulnerability, not an accusation of misconduct.

## S

**Scope**
The explicit boundary of a research question, chapter, finding, or plan. Scope identifies what is included, excluded, and limited by population, system, setting, task, outcome, period, or assumption.

**Software engineering**
The discipline and practice concerned with creating, operating, maintaining, changing, and retiring software systems under technical and human constraints. This is a provisional working definition; Part I examines its boundaries and necessary distinctions.

**Sociotechnical system**
A system whose behavior and outcomes arise through interaction among technical components, people, processes, organizations, and environments. The term cautions against explaining software outcomes through code or tools alone.

**Standing engineering policy**
An accepted application record authorizing a recurring class of low-risk work within explicit scope, risk, verification, ownership, and review boundaries.

**Source**
An identifiable origin of information used in research, such as a study, specification, dataset, repository, interview, observation, incident report, or technical artifact.

**Synthesis**
The integration of evidence or findings into a broader answer while preserving material differences, disagreement, scope, and uncertainty.

## T

**Technical debt**
A concept describing future costs, constraints, or risks associated with past technical decisions and compromises. The definition, measurement, and limits of the concept remain subjects for research rather than assumptions fixed by this glossary.

**Traceability**
The ability to follow a path from a source or observation through appraisal, analysis, finding, confidence, implication, and any later decision that uses it.

**Transparency**
The availability and comprehensibility of information needed to inspect a process, system, claim, or decision. More information is not necessarily more useful transparency.

**Trust**
A context-dependent relationship in which one party accepts vulnerability or uncertainty based partly on expectations about another party or system. Part V will examine and refine this provisional definition for AI-assisted engineering.

**Trust calibration**
The degree to which a person's trust or reliance corresponds to the system's actual trustworthiness within the relevant context.

**Trustworthiness**
The properties and demonstrated behavior that make reliance justifiable within a defined context. Trustworthiness is distinct from whether a person feels trust or whether a system is widely adopted.

## U

**Uncertainty**
The recognized limitation in knowledge about facts, outcomes, causes, requirements, system state, or future conditions. Uncertainty may be reducible through investigation or irreducible within available constraints.

## V

**Validity**
The extent to which evidence, a measure, method, or inference supports the interpretation for which it is used. Different validity concerns apply to different research designs.

**Verification**
The process of checking whether an artifact, action, result, or claim satisfies defined requirements or is supported by relevant evidence. Verification must identify what was checked, against which criterion, and under which conditions.

**Version fit**
The degree to which evidence concerns the same or a sufficiently similar version of the system, model, tool, dataset, policy, or document named in a claim.

## Z

**Zelyq**
The open-source project that motivates this research and may later apply or test some of its implications. Zelyq is not treated as evidence for general software-engineering claims unless it is the documented object of a study.

**Zelyq engineering entry**
The primary application record for one bounded Zelyq problem and proposed response. It traces research and project evidence through alternatives, judgment, risk, authorization, implementation, and outcome evaluation.

## Maintaining the glossary

Add a term when it:

- appears across multiple chapters or governing documents;
- has a project-specific, ambiguous, or contested meaning;
- affects how evidence or findings are interpreted; or
- must remain consistent for cross-chapter traceability.

Do not use the glossary to settle a research question by definition. When research materially changes a term, update the definition, record the reason, and review dependent chapters.

Terms should remain alphabetical, concise enough for reference use, and linked to fuller treatment when a dedicated chapter exists.
