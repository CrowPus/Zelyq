# Research Methodology

Version: 1.0

# Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the research methodology used throughout The Engineering Research Book.

This methodology exists to ensure that research is:

- repeatable
- evidence-driven
- intellectually honest
- peer-reviewable
- useful for engineering decisions

Research should reduce uncertainty.

Not create confidence without evidence.

---

# Research Philosophy

The purpose of research is not to prove ideas.

The purpose of research is to discover reality.

Researchers should never begin with a desired conclusion.

Instead they should begin with curiosity.

Every hypothesis should be considered temporary until supported by sufficient evidence.

Research is a continuous process.

Not a final destination.

---

# The Research Lifecycle

Every research topic follows the same lifecycle.

```

Question

↓

Research Proposal

↓

Background Research

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Evidence Classification

↓

Critical Analysis

↓

Counterarguments

↓

Discussion

↓

Conclusions

↓

Design Implications

↓

Peer Review

↓

Revision

↓

Approval

↓

Continuous Review

```

Every chapter should follow this lifecycle.

No stage should be skipped.

---

# Stage One

## Define The Question

Every research project begins with one clearly defined question.

Good questions are specific.

Example

What activities consume most of a senior engineer's time?

Poor questions are vague.

Example

How can we improve software engineering?

If the question is unclear,

the research will also become unclear.

---

# Stage Two

## Research Proposal

Before collecting evidence,

the researcher should define:

Research Question

Why it matters

Expected outcome

Potential impact on Zelyq

Related chapters

Potential biases

Unknown assumptions

This proposal becomes the roadmap.

---

# Stage Three

## Background Research

Before collecting new evidence,

researchers should understand existing knowledge.

Possible sources include:

Academic literature

Industry reports

Engineering blogs

Conference presentations

Developer surveys

Open-source projects

Company engineering handbooks

Books

Technical specifications

Historical case studies

Do not assume the problem has never been studied.

---

# Stage Four

## Evidence Collection

Collect evidence broadly.

Avoid selecting only evidence that supports existing beliefs.

Researchers should actively search for disagreement.

Evidence may include:

Peer-reviewed papers

Benchmark studies

Engineering metrics

Developer interviews

Community discussions

Case studies

Incident reports

Company documentation

Historical examples

Open-source repositories

Surveys

User research

Whenever possible,

multiple independent sources should support important conclusions.

---

# Stage Five

## Evidence Classification

Evidence is assessed for the claim it supports, not ranked by publication type alone.

For every material claim, researchers should evaluate:

- relevance to the question
- methodological fit
- risk of bias
- directness
- consistency
- precision
- transparency and reproducibility
- independence
- recency when the subject changes over time

The resulting body of evidence receives a confidence rating of High, Moderate, Low, or Insufficient.

The definitions, assessment procedure, and reporting requirements are maintained in the [Evidence Standard](07-evidence-standard.md).

Never use weak evidence to support a strong conclusion.

---

# Stage Six

## Analysis

Analysis should answer:

What patterns exist?

What surprises us?

What assumptions were incorrect?

What evidence conflicts?

What remains uncertain?

Analysis interprets evidence.

It does not invent evidence.

---

# Stage Seven

## Counterarguments

Every chapter must include disagreement.

Researchers should actively search for competing explanations.

Questions include:

What evidence challenges this conclusion?

Could another explanation exist?

Would another researcher interpret this differently?

What assumptions are required?

Good research survives criticism.

Weak research avoids it.

---

# Stage Eight

## Conclusions

Conclusions must be proportional to the evidence.

Strong evidence supports stronger conclusions.

Weak evidence supports weaker conclusions.

Avoid certainty when uncertainty remains.

Researchers should state confidence levels whenever appropriate.

---

# Stage Nine

## Design Implications

Every research project should conclude by answering:

How should this influence Zelyq?

Possible outcomes include:

No product changes.

Minor design considerations.

Major architectural changes.

Additional research required.

Research does not automatically produce features.

Sometimes the correct decision is to do nothing.

---

# Stage Ten

## Peer Review

Every chapter should be reviewed independently.

Reviewers should evaluate:

Research quality

Evidence quality

Logical consistency

Missing perspectives

Unsupported conclusions

Potential bias

Writing clarity

A chapter should not be approved because reviewers agree.

It should be approved because the reasoning withstands criticism.

---

# Stage Eleven

## Continuous Review

Research should never become static.

New evidence may require:

Updated conclusions

Revised recommendations

Additional chapters

Merged research

Retired hypotheses

Changing conclusions is expected.

Not discouraged.

---

# Research Principles

Every researcher agrees to the following principles.

Question assumptions.

Collect evidence honestly.

Represent opposing views fairly.

Separate evidence from opinion.

Document uncertainty.

Prefer understanding over speed.

Protect long-term credibility.

Remain open to changing conclusions.

---

# Bias

Researchers should actively monitor common biases.

Examples include:

Confirmation bias

Recency bias

Selection bias

Authority bias

Survivorship bias

Availability bias

Whenever possible,

seek evidence that challenges your current position.

---

# Research Quality Checklist

Before submitting research,

ask:

Is the question clear?

Did I investigate existing knowledge?

Did I collect enough evidence?

Did I search for disagreement?

Did I identify assumptions?

Did I separate facts from opinions?

Do the conclusions follow logically?

Did I explain how this affects Zelyq?

Would another researcher reach similar conclusions?

If multiple answers are "No",

the research should continue.

---

# The Role Of AI

Artificial intelligence may assist researchers.

AI can help summarize.

Organize.

Compare.

Translate.

Generate questions.

Identify patterns.

AI should not replace critical thinking.

Researchers remain responsible for the quality of conclusions.

---

# Success

Successful research does not prove someone correct.

Successful research reduces uncertainty.

It helps people make better engineering decisions.

It creates understanding that compounds over time.

That is the purpose of this methodology.

---

# Closing Statement

The Engineering Research Book exists to improve software engineering through disciplined, transparent, and collaborative research.

Every chapter is part of a larger effort to understand not only how software is built, but how it should be built in the future.

This methodology exists to protect that mission.

Every contributor is responsible for maintaining its standards.
