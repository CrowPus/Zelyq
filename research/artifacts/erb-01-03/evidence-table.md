# The Engineering Research Book

## ERB-01-03 evidence table

Record version: 0.1

Status: Gate A verified initial evidence set

| Source | Decision setting and uncertainty | Method and result | Permitted use | Prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S001 | Feature delivery now versus uncertain future architectural time saving | Questionnaire choice task; 33 developers in two Greek companies; widespread but highly variable temporal discounting | Software-specific evidence that time horizon and uncertain delayed value can change stated preferences | Real project behavior is identical; discounting is irrational; one rate fits people or contexts |
| S002 | Replication of software-project intertemporal choices | Changed-population/experimenter replication with professional and student samples across countries; reproduced discounting and high individual variation; broader experience associated with less discounting | Replication support for the existence and heterogeneity of the stated-preference effect | Experience causes superior decisions; students and professionals behave identically; the elicitation model explains mechanism |
| S003 | Technical-debt priority under technical and business perspectives | Two large-company cases with 22 senior participants across roles; mapping debt to configuration items and business processes changed priority ordering and supported discussion | Evidence that stakeholder perspective and decision representation can change what appears urgent or critical | Business-process modeling improves outcomes generally; business priorities should override technical risk |
| S004 | Uncertainty expressed in task-system discussions around architectural decisions | Three projects, selected discussion tasks, and five interviews; expressions served doubt, politeness, preference, assumption, and feedback functions and were sometimes interpreted differently | Evidence that uncertainty is communicated socially and can expose alternatives or invite clarification | Linguistic hedges measure objective uncertainty; uncertainty expression improves decision quality |
| S005 | Assumptions across software development | Systematic mapping of 134 studies from 2001–2015; only 21 defined assumption; emphasis on requirements/design, making/description/evaluation; maintenance thinner; context-limited evidence | Evidence map showing assumptions as accepted-without-evidence knowledge and uneven management coverage | Assumption management is proven effective; implicit assumptions caused a measured prevalence of failures |
| S009 | Architecture choices affecting multiple quality attributes | SEI normative method and worked example: stakeholder scenarios, attribute analyses, sensitivities, risks, non-risks, and tradeoff points iteratively refine candidate architectures | Definition and structured representation of architectural tradeoffs and decision visibility | ATAM use guarantees a good architecture or quantifies every important consequence |
| S010 | Evaluation of one avionics reference architecture | Two phases with 12 and 15 stakeholders; 15 scenarios analyzed; utility tree and risk/tradeoff records produced; authors report risk visibility and stakeholder cohesion | Bounded evidence that a structured method can produce shared artifacts and surface issues in one setting | The method caused better operational outcomes; the case generalizes to ordinary projects |

## Balance and dependence

- S001/S002 support temporal preference effects but use stylized stated-choice tasks.
- S003/S004 expose stakeholder framing and communication but are small qualitative case sets.
- S005 maps research activity rather than aggregating intervention effects.
- S009 is prescriptive and S010 is a method-family case report; neither supplies a controlled effectiveness comparison.
- S006–S008 remain access-pending and cannot silently support requirements-uncertainty claims.
