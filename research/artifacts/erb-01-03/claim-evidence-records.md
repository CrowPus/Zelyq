# The Engineering Research Book

## ERB-01-03 claim–evidence records

Record version: 0.1

Status: Gate B reviewed

### CER-01 — Uncertainty has multiple operational forms

Finding: In the included evidence, uncertainty appears as incomplete or unproven knowledge, ambiguous interpretation, uncertain future value, and uncertain quality consequences. Treating all four as one probability would erase decision-relevant differences.

Support: S001, S002, S004, S005, S009. Confidence: **Moderate**.

Prohibited inference: this is an exhaustive universal taxonomy.

### CER-02 — Constraints and priorities partly construct the tradeoff

Finding: What counts as preferable depends on which time horizon, stakeholder, quality, business process, risk, and resource boundary is represented. A tradeoff is therefore not fully specified by listing technical alternatives alone.

Support: S001, S003, S009, S010. Confidence: **Moderate**.

Prohibited inference: stakeholder preference is sufficient evidence or business priorities always dominate technical concerns.

### CER-03 — Intertemporal preferences vary substantially

Finding: In the original and replication questionnaire studies, participants commonly discounted delayed software-project benefits, with large individual variation and sensitivity to time horizon.

Support: S001 and S002. Confidence: **Moderate for the elicited tasks; Low for real project behavior**.

Prohibited inference: a stable personal discount rate exists, discounting is necessarily irrational, or the studies prove technical-debt causation.

### CER-04 — Visibility artifacts expose but do not settle decisions

Finding: Business-process mappings, discussion records, scenarios, utility trees, sensitivity points, and risk/tradeoff lists can expose assumptions, stakeholders, alternatives, or consequences. Their outputs still depend on selection, interpretation, facilitation, and available evidence.

Support: S003, S004, S009, S010; qualification from S005. Confidence: **Moderate–Low**.

Prohibited inference: using a named method or recording rationale makes a decision correct.

### CER-05 — Assumptions require lifecycle attention

Finding: The mapped literature concentrates on making, describing, and evaluating assumptions in requirements and design, while maintenance receives less attention; assumptions can lose validity as systems and environments change.

Support: S005. Confidence: **Moderate for the research map; Low for outcome effects**.

Prohibited inference: assumption management has proven general effectiveness or every implicit assumption is harmful.
