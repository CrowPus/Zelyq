# ERB-02-01 analysis notes

## Synthesis

- **Work is a portfolio, not a coding interval:** Observed work includes framing, locating information, understanding behavior, design, implementation, testing, coordination, review, release, and interruption recovery. Activity labels overlap and their proportions vary by role, phase, organization, and measurement.
- **Activities produce different evidence:** A code change demonstrates construction, not that the need was correctly framed, alternatives were examined, dependencies understood, behavior verified, or operational consequences observed. Each activity requires evidence matched to its purpose.
- **Coordination can be productive work:** Meetings, questions, and communication can interrupt focused construction, but field evidence also shows that they supply information, manage dependencies, and support planning or release. Counting all communication as waste erases its engineering function.
- **Agency and continuity affect work:** Self-report evidence links good workdays to progress and control over planned work. This supports treating fragmentation and blocked information as working conditions, not personal defects; it does not establish a universal productivity formula.

## Sensitivity and gaps

Removing self-report evidence leaves only observed, experimental, or artifact-based claims; any claim that would then weaken is rated no higher than Moderate. Removing student evidence prevents direct effectiveness generalization but does not remove professional-practice observations. The chapter does not establish universal prevalence, one best process, or causal superiority unless a listed design directly permits it. Underrepresented contexts include safety-critical work, small and non-Western organizations, accessibility, security, and longitudinal outcome comparisons.
