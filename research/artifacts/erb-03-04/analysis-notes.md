# ERB-03-04 analysis notes

## Mechanism synthesis

- **Tacit knowledge includes discrimination and context:** Engineers may know which signal matters, whom to ask, which invariant is fragile, or why an apparent option failed without being able to recover it from formal artifacts.
- **Teams maintain a map of who knows what:** Transactive memory can route questions efficiently, but it creates dependence on availability and accurate expertise attribution. A named owner is not proof that knowledge is transferable.
- **Transfer requires joint action and external traces:** Conversation, pairing, review, observation, and guided tasks can transfer situated knowledge; tests, decisions, runbooks, examples, and histories preserve inspectable fragments. Documentation alone cannot encode every skill or context.
- **Loss appears as uncertainty and delayed error:** Personnel departure, stale attribution, fragmented records, or inaccessible communication increases search, repeated decisions, unsafe assumptions, and inability to explain behavior. The magnitude varies and cannot be inferred from headcount alone.

## Sensitivity and unresolved evidence

Removing self-report reduces confidence but leaves direct observational, repository, case, or experimental support where listed. Removing single-organization sources narrows claims to mechanisms rather than prevalence. Correlations are not interpreted as causes, practice accounts are not effectiveness tests, and artifact completion is not treated as an outcome. Longitudinal causal comparisons and diverse organizational contexts remain sparse.
