# ERB-02-06 analysis notes

## Synthesis

- **Risk statements need structure:** A usable risk identifies a valued objective or harm, uncertain condition or mechanism, exposure/context, possible consequence, evidence, and owner. A score without these fields can conceal disagreement and false precision.
- **Uncertainty reduction and risk acceptance differ:** Experiments, prototypes, monitoring, reviews, and analysis can reduce particular uncertainties. Residual risk requires an accountable acceptance decision; performing an activity does not silently accept consequences for affected parties.
- **Management effects depend on integration and communication:** The reviewed literature does not support a universal method-effect claim. Benefits depend on risks becoming connected to goals, decisions, action, escalation, and feedback rather than existing only in a register.
- **Unknown and interacting hazards limit checklists:** Cases show that omitted software hazards, shared assumptions, organizational response, and defense interaction can dominate. Risk work must search for missing models and correlated failure, not only rank known items.

## Sensitivity and gaps

Removing self-report evidence leaves only observed, experimental, or artifact-based claims; any claim that would then weaken is rated no higher than Moderate. Removing student evidence prevents direct effectiveness generalization but does not remove professional-practice observations. The chapter does not establish universal prevalence, one best process, or causal superiority unless a listed design directly permits it. Underrepresented contexts include safety-critical work, small and non-Western organizations, accessibility, security, and longitudinal outcome comparisons.
