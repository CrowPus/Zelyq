# ERB-02-07 analysis notes

## Synthesis

- **Experience is exposure; expertise is demonstrated performance:** Years, title, reputation, and self-identification are proxies that mix opportunity, domain familiarity, role, and selection. They cannot replace task-relevant observation.
- **Reliable judgment makes its basis testable:** Stronger observable practice includes framing the problem, recognizing cues and limits, generating or checking alternatives when warranted, seeking disconfirming evidence, calibrating confidence, and revising after feedback.
- **Transfer is conditional:** Performance can fail to transfer across domains, languages, architectures, organizations, tools, task types, and consequence levels. Familiar patterns accelerate work but can also anchor an incorrect frame.
- **Feedback quality shapes learning:** Repetition without timely, valid outcome feedback can reinforce weak habits. Evidence of learning requires improved discrimination or outcomes, not merely accumulated activity.

## Sensitivity and gaps

Removing self-report evidence leaves only observed, experimental, or artifact-based claims; any claim that would then weaken is rated no higher than Moderate. Removing student evidence prevents direct effectiveness generalization but does not remove professional-practice observations. The chapter does not establish universal prevalence, one best process, or causal superiority unless a listed design directly permits it. Underrepresented contexts include safety-critical work, small and non-Western organizations, accessibility, security, and longitudinal outcome comparisons.
