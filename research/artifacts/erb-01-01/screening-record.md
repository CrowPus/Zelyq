## Full-text decisions

### ERB-01-01-S008 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the study directly observed 14 developers across approximately 51 hours in two sub-teams of a large Brazilian organization and used pre- and post-observation interviews. It records collaboration, information seeking, communication media, tools, and interruptions during actual work.

Restriction: one researcher observed one collocated organizational setting, participants contributed only one to three work shifts each, and activity classification involved researcher judgment. Percentages are not population estimates and may depend on the organization's process, physical layout, projects, and observation period.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S009 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the report combines two surveys of randomly selected Microsoft job-title groups with 11 semi-structured interviews. It directly examines code comprehension, rationale, mental models, coordination, code ownership, documentation, onboarding, and interruptions.

Restriction: the evidence comes from one company in 2005, response rates were limited, and the published technical report presents selected observations rather than the full instrument and dataset. Surveys and interviews measure participant reports and perceptions, not directly observed behavior or product outcomes. The authors report an unresolved conflict between interview and survey accounts of design-document use.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S003 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the joint ACM/IEEE Computer Society guidelines directly discuss the software-engineering discipline, its relationship to programming, expected graduate competencies, professional practice, design, verification, teamwork, stakeholder communication, and the range of education knowledge areas. The document reports a revision process involving a 477-response survey across 42 countries, workshops, public review, and expert review.

Restriction: this is a curriculum recommendation and professional framing document. Stakeholder input informed its development, but the resulting framework is not an empirical description of all practitioners, a comparison of occupational groups, or evidence that possessing a listed competency causes a particular project outcome. It was published in 2015 and reflects undergraduate educational goals.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S004 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the primary conference report documents why the term “software engineering” was selected, the scope used to organize the conference, and contemporary debates about large-system design, production, coordination, documentation, verification, distribution, updating, and service.

Restriction: the report preserves edited working papers and attributed discussion. Its editors explicitly state that disagreements were left open and that they did not seek a consensus or majority view. It is historical and argumentative evidence, not a representative survey or causal study. The searchable edition also warns that scanning and optical character recognition may have introduced errors.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S001 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the current official IEEE Computer Society body-of-knowledge guide is directly relevant to definitions, disciplinary scope, lifecycle responsibilities, and the relationship between theory, practice, and engineering economics. Its relevant content and development basis are inspectable.

Restriction: use as a normative and consensus framework. It does not establish how all practitioners actually work or prove that a responsibility causes an outcome.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S006 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the study directly measured computer activity and collected repeated self-reports from professional developers in four companies. It distinguishes coding, planning, documentation, review, communication, meetings, information seeking, and other activities, making it relevant to the observable scope of development work.

Restriction: the analyzed sample contained 20 participants, monitoring could not identify all off-computer or remote-desktop activity, and perceived productivity is not objective product or system performance. The participants were called developers; the study did not compare independently defined programmers and software engineers.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S007 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: 5,971 workday responses from professional Microsoft developers report time spent across coding, bug fixing, testing, specification, review, documentation, collaboration, helping, and learning. The study also relates those activities to self-assessed good and typical workdays.

Restriction: evidence comes from one organization and self-reported prior-day activity. Responses may include repeat participants, and perceived workday quality is not an objective software outcome. The study does not independently define or compare programmer and software-engineer populations.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S005 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the full author paper directly discusses the origin and contested meaning of “software engineering,” the distinction from programming, lifecycle scope, multi-person and multi-version work, and differences between software-production contexts.

Restriction: use as an author retrospective and conceptual/historical interpretation. It is not an empirical comparative study and cannot establish general prevalence or causal outcome effects.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S010 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the repository-mining study directly examines relationships between review coverage, participation, reviewer expertise, and post-release defect counts across four releases of three large open-source systems.

Restriction: the design is observational, uses repository-derived proxies, and cannot establish that review properties caused defect differences. Results varied across releases, and coverage alone was not consistently explanatory.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S011 — Include as material qualifying evidence

Decision: include.

Reason: the study reproduces and extends S010 with Qt and six Chrome releases, tests sensitivity to variable selection, applies alternative Bayesian-network models, and provides an open replication package. It directly evaluates whether the original review-measure associations are stable.

Restriction: it remains an observational repository study. Bayesian-network structure does not by itself establish causal effects, and operational measures differ where project data required adaptation.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S012 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: this peer-reviewed study reports a globally distributed family of surveys covering 228 organizations in 10 countries and directly examines requirements problems, practitioner-attributed causes, perceived effects, and contextual variation.

Restriction: respondents reported perceptions rather than independently measured project events. The sample's representativeness is unknown, domains are unevenly distributed, the questionnaire constrains responses, and the cause-effect representation does not demonstrate causality.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S014 — Include with restricted use

Decision: include.

Reason: the study systematically synthesizes 27 comparative studies of test-driven development and evaluates both external quality and productivity, including subgroup and moderator analyses.

Restriction: primary studies use heterogeneous tasks, participants, controls, and outcome measures. Standardized data were often unavailable, industrial evidence was limited, and publication bias could not be assessed meaningfully.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S015 — Include as qualifying evidence

Decision: include.

Reason: the study evaluates test presence, executability, static properties, dynamic properties, and test smells against post-release defects in eight Apache Commons systems while adding production and process confounders.

Restriction: this is an observational case study of unit-test-related repository measures in one project family. Its findings do not measure all testing work, test design quality, integration or system testing, or causal effects.

Review requirement: independent assessment is required before the source supports a central finding.

### ERB-01-01-S016 — Exclude after full-text review

Decision: exclude from evidentiary synthesis.

Reason: the report distinguishes programmer, software developer, software engineer, and systems analyst titles in 1,291 positions advertised through *Computerworld* between 1990 and 2004. Its substantive coding, however, concerns requested programming languages, operating systems, databases, networking, and distributed technologies. It does not compare lifecycle responsibilities, decision authority, observed work, professional accountability, or outcomes between title groups.

Exclusion implication: the changing frequency of titles cannot establish a change in responsibility, and differences in selected technical requirements cannot establish the broader distinction asked by ERB-01-01. The source remains in the inventory to make this boundary-search decision auditable.
