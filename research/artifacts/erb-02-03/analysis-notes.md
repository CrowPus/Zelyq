# ERB-02-03 analysis notes

## Synthesis

- **Requirements are discovered and negotiated, not merely received:** Stakeholders may hold partial, tacit, conflicting, or changing knowledge. Elicitation is an inquiry into goals, work, constraints, vocabulary, and consequences rather than transcription of requests.
- **The problem boundary is a decision:** Who counts as a stakeholder, which environment is modeled, and which outcomes or harms are considered determine what becomes a requirement. Excluded perspectives remain a source of uncertainty.
- **Representations are probes:** Scenarios, prototypes, models, examples, and acceptance conditions can reveal disagreement and unknowns. Agreement with a representation is evidence about that interaction, not permanent validation of the need.
- **Traceability must continue downstream:** Framing loses value if its assumptions and acceptance conditions cannot be connected to design, implementation, verification, and later change. Case evidence supports the importance of cross-role communication but not one universal process.

## Sensitivity and gaps

Removing self-report evidence leaves only observed, experimental, or artifact-based claims; any claim that would then weaken is rated no higher than Moderate. Removing student evidence prevents direct effectiveness generalization but does not remove professional-practice observations. The chapter does not establish universal prevalence, one best process, or causal superiority unless a listed design directly permits it. Underrepresented contexts include safety-critical work, small and non-Western organizations, accessibility, security, and longitudinal outcome comparisons.
