# ERB-03-05 analysis notes

## Mechanism synthesis

- **Onboarding is staged participation, not document consumption:** A newcomer must locate people and artifacts, build system and workflow models, learn norms, obtain access, choose a tractable task, make a change, and receive valid feedback.
- **The first task is an evidence instrument:** A bounded real task can reveal whether the contributor can navigate, predict impact, follow governance, verify work, communicate uncertainty, and recover. Trivial setup proves little; an oversized task confounds knowledge with scope.
- **Social and technical barriers interact:** Missing documentation, tooling failures, unclear contribution paths, delayed response, fear of error, unfamiliar vocabulary, and unavailable mentors can reinforce one another. Blaming the newcomer hides system conditions.
- **Permission should expand with demonstrated understanding:** Safe onboarding can use progressive scope, review, reversible environments, and consequence-aware access. Completion time alone is not readiness; evidence should match the responsibilities being granted.

## Sensitivity and unresolved evidence

Removing self-report reduces confidence but leaves direct observational, repository, case, or experimental support where listed. Removing single-organization sources narrows claims to mechanisms rather than prevalence. Correlations are not interpreted as causes, practice accounts are not effectiveness tests, and artifact completion is not treated as an outcome. Longitudinal causal comparisons and diverse organizational contexts remain sparse.
