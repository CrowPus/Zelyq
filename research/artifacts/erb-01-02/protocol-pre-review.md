## Alignment review

Decision: **Ready for protocol approval review after corrections**

The design addresses the approved primary question through definition, element-and-relationship, mechanism-and-outcome, cross-level and temporal, and alternative-explanation analysis. It preserves the boundaries with ERB-01-03, ERB-01-04, ERB-01-05, AI research, and Zelyq engineering application.

The protocol does not presume that one sociotechnical definition will be accepted. Its operational boundary requires identifiable technical and nontechnical elements, a relationship, a connected behavior or consequence, and an adequate system boundary and level of analysis.

## Search and screening review

The source classes, five concept groups, initial queries, supplementary discovery, eligibility rules, screening sequence, coverage controls, and independent Gate A checks are adequate for a structured synthesis.

Two material omissions were identified and corrected.

### PPR-01 — Search imbalance toward failure

Concern: the initial strings included a dedicated incident and failure query but no comparable query for ordinary work, successful adaptation, workarounds, resilience, or recovery. This could amplify failure and hindsight bias despite the proposal's stated mitigation.

Correction: added a dedicated adaptation and ordinary-work search string combining lifecycle work, adaptation terms, and human or organizational contexts.

Status: Resolved in protocol version 0.2.

### PPR-02 — Undefined search stopping point

Concern: the protocol described pilot and supplementary discovery but did not state when the search could stop. Without a recorded completion rule, stopping could become sensitive to emerging conclusions.

Correction: added execution, disposition, citation-checking, contrary-search, coverage, and access-gap completion conditions. Supplementary search now requires two consecutive documented rounds without a new central construct, mechanism, material contradiction, or source class.

Status: Resolved in protocol version 0.2.

## Evidence and causal-language review

Decision: **Ready for protocol approval review**

The extraction record separates source definitions, elements, relationships, levels, paths of responsibility and information, outcomes, methods, alternatives, limitations, permitted uses, and prohibited inferences. The appraisal plan tests construct validity, system boundaries, level mismatch, temporality, reciprocal effects, selection, hindsight, proxy validity, and source dependence.

The mechanism definition does not classify plausibility, sequence, association, or testimony as causal evidence. Incident accounts cannot establish prevalence, and self-reports remain bounded to reports or perceptions unless corroborated by an appropriate method.

## Domain-boundary review

Decision: **Ready with questions for the approving reviewer**

The protocol treats technical and nontechnical categories as analytical aids rather than necessarily independent causes. It permits relational and mutually shaping accounts and requires comparison with technical-only, process-only, organizational, ecological, and other explanations.

The approving reviewer should still decide whether:

- “software-intensive system” is sufficiently bounded for screening across adjacent disciplines;
- the proposed coding dimensions omit a material kind of element or relationship;
- the incident and safety evidence plan remains proportionate to this chapter rather than ERB-01-05; and
- two search rounds without conceptual novelty provide a reasonable supplementary stopping rule.

## Ethics, privacy, security, and licensing review

Decision: **Adequate for published-source research**

The protocol authorizes no participant recruitment, private-data collection, or systematic analysis of identifiable communications. It minimizes personal and security-sensitive details, prohibits submitting restricted material to AI, and requires an amendment plus applicable review before the research mode changes.

## Record and reproducibility review

Decision: **Ready for approval review**

The protocol defines required search, screening, source, evidence, coding, comparison, claim, analysis, amendment, and review records. It prohibits empty records from implying progress. Search strings, source locations, dates, result counts, dispositions, versions, dependencies, deviations, and material AI use must remain auditable.

## Pre-review conclusion

Protocol version 0.2 is ready for a human approval decision. This pre-review resolved two design defects but does not authorize evidence collection. Approval must be recorded in the review record, after which the execution records can be created and the chapter can move to `In research`.
