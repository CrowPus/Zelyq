# ERB-05-01 analysis notes

## Synthesis

- **Trust is an attitude; reliance is behavior:** A person may feel confident in a system but reject its advice, or rely on it under policy or workload while distrusting it. Attitude surveys cannot substitute for observed acceptance, verification, delegation, or intervention.
- **Trustworthiness is evidence-relative system quality:** For this book, trustworthiness means possessing and demonstrating properties that justify bounded reliance: validity, reliability, security, accountability, transparency where useful, controllability, and recovery in the intended context.
- **Calibration concerns conditional reliance:** Appropriate reliance means using correct assistance and rejecting or escalating incorrect or out-of-scope assistance under stated conditions. High acceptance, satisfaction, adoption, or trust is not the target.
- **Reliance belongs to a sociotechnical arrangement:** Users rely on a configured model, tools, organization, controls, evidence, and responsible people—not an abstract model alone. Institutional arrangements can be trustworthy even where interpersonal trust in software is conceptually disputed.

## Sensitivity and gaps

Removing non-engineering experiments lowers directness but leaves human-factors mechanisms and engineering-specific risk boundaries. Removing first-party frameworks leaves experimental findings but weakens governance vocabulary. Mixed explanation and collaboration results are preserved rather than averaged into a universal recommendation. Evidence remains thin for longitudinal software teams, accessibility, power differences, organizational incentives, field incidents, and skill retention.
