## Primary research question

> Which conclusions can Version 1 defend, where do they not generalize, and which questions require further research?

## Findings

### Defensible conclusion: engineering exceeds code production

Across Parts I–III, software engineering is continuing sociotechnical responsibility for purpose, evidence, uncertainty, change, operation, coordination, and learning.

### Defensible conclusion: AI capability is real but bounded

As of August 25, 2026, configured systems demonstrate multiple useful engineering-task capabilities, but evidence does not establish reliable autonomous lifecycle responsibility.

### Defensible conclusion: reliance must be conditional

Task evidence, calibrated uncertainty, enforced authorization, meaningful oversight, recovery, accountability, and continuing validation are required in proportion to consequence.

### Non-generalization: benchmark to field

Public task success does not directly estimate production reliability, organizational fit, user impact, security, maintenance, or longitudinal learning.

### Non-generalization: one domain or population

Evidence overrepresents public repositories, Python, technology organizations, bounded online experiments, English interaction, and accessible published outcomes.

### Open agenda: causal field evidence

Priority questions include longitudinal developer/team outcomes, skill and knowledge change, incident rates, verification load, organizational incentives, small and non-Western settings, accessibility, privacy, energy/cost, and rare high-consequence failure.

### Open agenda: evaluation science

Needed work includes contamination-resistant living tasks, validated oracles, realistic adversarial environments, consequence-aware measures, correlated-failure analysis, memory update/deletion, oversight capacity, and post-deployment recovery.

### Open agenda: governance and affected parties

Research must examine who defines purposes and thresholds, how power and consent shape data and delegation, how people contest outcomes, and how accountability survives vendor and model change.

## Approved synthesis findings

1. **Version 1 supports a bounded responsibility/capability/reliance framework — High within recorded scope:** trace to Parts I–V; do not infer the framework is a product specification.
2. **Reviewed evidence does not justify autonomous end-to-end engineering reliance — High as of cutoff:** trace to ERB-04-S, ERB-05-S; do not infer autonomy is impossible.
3. **Field, population, language, and publication coverage limit generalization — High:** trace to all chapter limitations; do not infer no conclusions transfer beyond samples.
4. **The open agenda prioritizes evidence capable of changing central conclusions — Moderate:** trace to cross-part gap synthesis; do not infer listed questions are equally urgent everywhere.

## Use boundary

These findings define research-grounded evaluation structure. They are not a product specification, architecture, model selection, backlog, or authorization to implement. A separate engineering agent must apply them to verified project context through the governance process.

## Limitations

This chapter inherits every material upstream limitation and adds synthesis risk: categories may simplify interacting responsibilities, and operationalizations require validation in the intended context. No synthesis confidence exceeds its weakest material premise.
