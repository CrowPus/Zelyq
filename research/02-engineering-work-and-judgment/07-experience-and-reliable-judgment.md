## Primary research question

> Which observable practices distinguish more reliable engineering judgment, and under what conditions does experience fail to transfer?

## Findings

### Experience is exposure; expertise is demonstrated performance

Years, title, reputation, and self-identification are proxies that mix opportunity, domain familiarity, role, and selection. They cannot replace task-relevant observation.

### Reliable judgment makes its basis testable

Stronger observable practice includes framing the problem, recognizing cues and limits, generating or checking alternatives when warranted, seeking disconfirming evidence, calibrating confidence, and revising after feedback.

### Transfer is conditional

Performance can fail to transfer across domains, languages, architectures, organizations, tools, task types, and consequence levels. Familiar patterns accelerate work but can also anchor an incorrect frame.

### Feedback quality shapes learning

Repetition without timely, valid outcome feedback can reinforce weak habits. Evidence of learning requires improved discrimination or outcomes, not merely accumulated activity.

## Evaluation consequence

Later capability or trust research must convert these findings into observable, task-specific evidence. Neither fluent explanation, process compliance, artifact count, tenure, nor successful code production is sufficient by itself. The required evidence depends on purpose, uncertainty, consequence, and operating context.

## Limitations

This is a structured, non-exhaustive synthesis. Study settings, constructs, and outcomes differ; several sources use self-report, small samples, students, or organization-specific data. The findings identify defensible mechanisms and boundaries, not universal activity rates or a single best method.

## Research boundary

This chapter does not inspect Zelyq, define an AI product, select an implementation, or authorize code.
