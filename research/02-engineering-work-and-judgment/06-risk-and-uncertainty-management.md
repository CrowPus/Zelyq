## Primary research question

> How do engineers recognize, communicate, reduce, and accept technical risk and uncertainty?

## Findings

### Risk statements need structure

A usable risk identifies a valued objective or harm, uncertain condition or mechanism, exposure/context, possible consequence, evidence, and owner. A score without these fields can conceal disagreement and false precision.

### Uncertainty reduction and risk acceptance differ

Experiments, prototypes, monitoring, reviews, and analysis can reduce particular uncertainties. Residual risk requires an accountable acceptance decision; performing an activity does not silently accept consequences for affected parties.

### Management effects depend on integration and communication

The reviewed literature does not support a universal method-effect claim. Benefits depend on risks becoming connected to goals, decisions, action, escalation, and feedback rather than existing only in a register.

### Unknown and interacting hazards limit checklists

Cases show that omitted software hazards, shared assumptions, organizational response, and defense interaction can dominate. Risk work must search for missing models and correlated failure, not only rank known items.

## Evaluation consequence

Later capability or trust research must convert these findings into observable, task-specific evidence. Neither fluent explanation, process compliance, artifact count, tenure, nor successful code production is sufficient by itself. The required evidence depends on purpose, uncertainty, consequence, and operating context.

## Limitations

This is a structured, non-exhaustive synthesis. Study settings, constructs, and outcomes differ; several sources use self-report, small samples, students, or organization-specific data. The findings identify defensible mechanisms and boundaries, not universal activity rates or a single best method.
