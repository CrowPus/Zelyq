## Part I — The nature of software engineering

Part ID: ERB-01

Version: 0.2

## Central research question

> What is software engineering, and why does it remain difficult even when producing code becomes easier?

## Purpose

Part I establishes what the book means by software engineering before it evaluates engineers, teams, AI capabilities, or trustworthy collaboration.

The part does not begin from the premise that software engineering is categorically separate from programming. It investigates how relevant communities define the activities, where their boundaries overlap, and whether the distinction changes how responsibilities and outcomes should be understood.

## Included scope

This part examines:

- the objects and objectives of software engineering;
- the relationship between programming and wider lifecycle responsibilities;
- software as a changing sociotechnical system;
- uncertainty, constraint, and tradeoffs;
- the forms of understanding required to change software responsibly; and
- mechanisms through which software remains effective, degrades, or fails over time.

## Excluded scope

This part does not determine:

- which responsibilities an AI system should perform;
- whether AI can replace or equal human engineers;
- which features Zelyq should implement;
- how Zelyq should be architected; or
- a universal professional or legal definition of who may use the title “software engineer.”

Those questions either belong to later parts or require separate project-specific application records.

## Chapter sequence

| ID | Chapter | Role in the part |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ERB-01-01 | Software engineering and programming | Establish the relationship and consequential distinctions between the activities |
| ERB-01-02 | Software as a sociotechnical system | Examine interaction among software, people, process, organization, and environment |
| ERB-01-03 | Uncertainty, constraints, and tradeoffs | Identify conditions under which engineering decisions are made |
| ERB-01-04 | The problem of system understanding | Determine which forms of understanding engineering work requires and why they become difficult |
| ERB-01-05 | Success, degradation, and failure over time | Examine mechanisms affecting software across its lifecycle |

The sequence begins with definitions and responsibilities, then examines the system being engineered, the decision environment, the understanding needed to act, and the consequences observed over time.

## Dependencies

Part I depends on the book's methodology and evidence standard but does not depend on findings from later research parts.

Later parts may depend on Part I when they define engineering work, evaluate AI capabilities, or determine what trustworthy participation requires.

## Synthesis plan

The part will synthesize reviewed chapter findings into:

- a bounded working account of software engineering;
- a map of responsibilities and contexts that later chapters must consider;
- a record of disputed or context-dependent distinctions;
- a vocabulary for software systems, change, uncertainty, and understanding; and
- open questions that prevent overgeneralization.

The synthesis will not convert descriptive findings into Zelyq requirements. Project application follows the research-to-code governance process.

## Exit criterion

Part I is complete when it provides a defensible, evidence-based account of software engineering that:

- identifies its relationship to programming without relying on occupational prestige;
- represents technical, human, organizational, and lifecycle responsibilities;
- states where definitions and evidence disagree;
- distinguishes established findings from working concepts;
- records confidence and limitations; and
- does not depend on a preferred AI or Zelyq design.
