## Primary research question

> How do technical artifacts, people, processes, organizations, and environments interact in the behavior and evolution of software systems?

## Current state

Corrective discovery was approved by Dee Empire. The auditable ledger contains 505 returned-result records and 159 candidates. Gate A accepted nine of 11 included extractions after complete-text inspection. S002 and S130 were inaccessible and remain `Unable to verify`; the synthesis was narrowed so neither is load-bearing. The remaining candidates form a documented reserve set rather than an assumed body of support. Gate B accepted four bounded findings and retained the former cross-level claim only as an open hypothesis.

Protocol approval does not establish that every software system should be analyzed through one sociotechnical model, approve a Zelyq design, or authorize code.

## Analysis

The [interaction coding record](../artifacts/erb-01-02/interaction-coding-record.md) treats the relationship—not the mere presence of technical and social elements—as the unit of analysis. Seven recurring patterns were compared across development, coordination, delivery, operation, and high-hazard settings. Technical-only, process-only, organizational, ecological, and measurement explanations remain visible in the [analysis notes](../artifacts/erb-01-02/analysis-notes.md).

## Findings

The reviewed synthesis supports four bounded findings:

1. In the studied software settings, technical dependencies become behaviorally consequential through work allocation, expertise, communication, coordination, and feedback. A dependency graph or organization chart alone does not describe how the work is accomplished.
2. Coordination artifacts—including shared views, histories, chat, logs, and monitoring—shape awareness by selecting and organizing information. Their usefulness depends on task, role, scale, timing, interpretability, privacy, and information cost; more visibility is not automatically better.
3. Architecture, deployment practices, team responsibilities, operational roles, and coordination arrangements co-adjust over time. The evidence does not support one universally optimal stable alignment.
4. A sociotechnical explanation is useful only when it specifies elements, relationships, boundary, level, temporal path, and outcome. A large 25-project study found no meaningful relationship between one congruence operationalization and bugs or churn, preventing any general claim that sociotechnical congruence predicts software quality.

Detailed support, qualifications, confidence, and prohibited inferences are in the [claim–evidence records](../artifacts/erb-01-02/claim-evidence-records.md). CER-04, concerning divergence between local reliability and system-level effects, remains an open hypothesis because its strongest source was `Unable to verify`.

## Implications

No code authorization is created by this chapter state. An engineering entry may cite the four reviewed findings, but must separately verify project context, alternatives, risks, boundaries, evaluation, and authorization. Any translation must remain traceable to bounded claims and preserve contrary evidence.

## Limitations and open questions

The evidence set is deliberately heterogeneous and cannot be pooled as a single effect. Most direct software studies are observational, exploratory, or practitioner-reported. Individual-level, public-sector, deployment/evolution, and independent causal evidence remain thinner than team and organization evidence. S002 and S130 remain `Unable to verify`; several priority sources remain access-pending. The 148 candidates outside the initial included set are deferred, not rejected. Material AI involvement, access paths, and limitations remain disclosed in the artifacts.
